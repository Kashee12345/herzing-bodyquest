INTERVENTIONS · DIALS

OSMOTHERAPYOFF
Mannitol / hypertonic saline pulls water out of the brain. The definitive way to reduce the swelling and the ICP.
HYPERVENTILATIONOFF
Lowers PaCO₂ → constricts cerebral vessels → drops ICP fast. But overdo it and you starve the brain. A bridge, never a cure — check the gas.
VASOPRESSOR (MAP support)OFF
Raises the MAP to protect CPP (= MAP − ICP). The other half of the battle — do not let perfusion fall while chasing the pressure.
CSF DRAIN (EVD) CLOSED
Open the ventricular drain to remove CSF directly — an immediate, definitive way to offload intracranial volume.

CASE DEBRIEF

THE COUPLED EQUATION — CPP = MAP − ICP

Blue is the perfusion the brain actually got (CPP). It is the gap between the gold line (MAP) and the pink line (ICP). Every time ICP rose or MAP sagged, that gap — the perfusion — was squeezed. The goal was to keep ICP under the top dashed line AND CPP above the bottom one, at the same time.

ICP MAP CPP

MONRO-KELLIE — THE FLAT LINE, THEN THE CLIFF

The shaded area is the hidden compensatory reserve — the skull's room to absorb swelling. While it lasted, the ICP (pink) barely moved as the mass (orange) grew. Watch what happens to the ICP once the reserve runs out: the same swelling now sends it straight up.

intracranial mass ICP Monro-Kellie reserve (hidden)

HYPERVENTILATION — THE DIAL THAT CUTS BOTH WAYS

Hyperventilation lowers PaCO₂ (blue) to shrink cerebral blood volume and drop ICP — but below the dashed line the vessels clamp so hard they starve the brain, and ischemia (pink) climbs. A bridge, not a treatment.
