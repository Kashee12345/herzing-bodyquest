💊 Dosage Calculation Formulas ▼
The golden rule: Always double-check your answer by estimating first. If the ordered dose is twice the on-hand concentration, you should give twice the quantity. Does your answer make intuitive sense?
Basic Dose Calculation
Dose = (Desired dose ÷ Dose on hand) × Quantity
D/H × Q. Most common formula in nursing practice. Works for tablets, liquids, and injectables.
IV Flow Rate (mL/hr)
Rate (mL/hr) = Volume (mL) ÷ Time (hr)
For infusion pumps. Always use hours. Example: 1000 mL over 8 hours = 125 mL/hr.
IV Drip Rate (gtt/min)
gtt/min = (Volume (mL) × Drop factor) ÷ Time (min)
Common drop factors: 10 gtt/mL, 15 gtt/mL, 20 gtt/mL (standard); 60 gtt/mL (microdrip).
Weight-Based Dosing
Dose = Weight (kg) × mg/kg ordered
Always convert lbs to kg first: kg = lbs ÷ 2.2. Then apply the mg/kg rate.
Percent Concentration
% = (Parts ÷ Whole) × 100
A 0.9% NaCl solution means 0.9g NaCl per 100 mL. A 5% dextrose = 5g glucose per 100 mL.
⚙️ Algebra Fundamentals ▼
Golden rule of equations: Whatever you do to one side, do to the other. The equation must stay balanced.
Solving for x (one step)
x + a = b → x = b − a x × a = b → x = b ÷ a x ÷ a = b → x = b × a
Identify what operation is applied to x, then do the inverse operation to both sides.
Solving for x (two steps)
ax + b = c → x = (c − b) ÷ a
Step 1: Subtract b from both sides. Step 2: Divide both sides by a. Always eliminate addition/subtraction first, then multiplication/division.
Proportions (Cross Multiply)
a/b = c/d → ad = bc → solve for unknown
Most powerful tool in nursing math. 250 mg/5 mL = x mg/10 mL → x = 500 mg.
Percentage Change
% Change = ((New − Old) ÷ Old) × 100
Positive = increase, negative = decrease. Clinical use: tracking weight change, lab value trends.
Linear Equation (Slope-Intercept)
y = mx + b (m = slope, b = y-intercept)
m = rise ÷ run = (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁). Clinical use: trending vital signs, growth charts.
📊 Statistics Formulas ▼
Remember MMMR: Mean, Median, Mode, Range. Each tells you something different about a data set.
Mean (Average)
Mean = Σx ÷ n = (sum of all values) ÷ (count)
Most affected by outliers. Best for symmetric distributions. Clinical use: average blood pressure across readings.
Median
Middle value when data is sorted in order. Even count: average the two middle values.
Resistant to outliers. Best for skewed distributions. Clinical use: median lab value when one reading is suspicious.
Mode
The value that appears most frequently.
A dataset can have 0, 1, or multiple modes. Clinical use: most common symptom reported by a patient group.
Range
Range = Maximum value − Minimum value
Simplest measure of spread. Clinical use: BP range across a shift, temperature fluctuation over 24 hours.
Identifying Outliers (Quick Method)
Outlier: value more than 1.5× IQR above Q3 or below Q1 Simple rule: value that is dramatically separated from the cluster
Clinical significance: an outlier in a lab value set may indicate an error, contamination, or a critical event worth investigating.

💊 Worked Example: Dosage Calculation ▼
Problem: Ordered: Amoxicillin 500 mg PO. Available: Amoxicillin 250 mg/5 mL suspension. How many mL do you give?
Step 1: Write out D/H × Q
Desired = 500 mg On hand = 250 mg per 5 mL Quantity = 5 mL
Always identify your three values before calculating.
Step 2: Set up the formula
(500 ÷ 250) × 5 = ?
Never skip this step — write it out. Errors happen when students try to do it mentally.
Step 3: Calculate
500 ÷ 250 = 2.0 2.0 × 5 = 10 mL
Answer: Give 10 mL. Sanity check: ordered is 2× on-hand, so give 2× quantity. ✓
💧 Worked Example: IV Flow Rate ▼
Problem: Ordered: 1,000 mL NS over 8 hours via infusion pump. What rate do you set (mL/hr)?
Step 1: Identify volume and time
Volume = 1,000 mL Time = 8 hours
Step 2: Apply the formula
Rate = Volume ÷ Time Rate = 1,000 ÷ 8 = 125 mL/hr
Answer: Set pump to 125 mL/hr. Sanity check: 125 × 8 = 1,000 mL. ✓
⚙️ Worked Example: Two-Step Equation ▼
Problem: A patient medication dose (x) is calculated as: 2x + 4 = 24. Find x.
Step 1: Subtract 4 from both sides
2x + 4 − 4 = 24 − 4 2x = 20
Eliminate the + 4 by subtracting 4. Goal: isolate x.
Step 2: Divide both sides by 2
2x ÷ 2 = 20 ÷ 2 x = 10
Answer: x = 10 mg. Check: 2(10) + 4 = 24 ✓
📊 Worked Example: MMMR from Patient Data ▼
Problem: A patient systolic BP was recorded 5 times: 118, 122, 118, 135, 127. Find mean, median, mode, and range.
Step 1: Sort the data first
118, 118, 122, 127, 135
Always sort before finding median and mode. Sorting prevents errors.
Step 2: Mean
(118 + 118 + 122 + 127 + 135) ÷ 5 = 620 ÷ 5 = 124
Step 3: Median
Sorted: 118, 118, [122], 127, 135 Middle value = 122
Step 4: Mode
118 appears twice — all others once. Mode = 118
Step 5: Range
135 − 118 = 17 Range = 17 mmHg
Clinical note: A range of 17 mmHg over 5 readings is moderate variability — worth monitoring.
